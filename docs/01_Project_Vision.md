# Project Vision

## Vision

Create an AI shopping advisor that helps users discover retailers, compare products, understand retailer tradeoffs, maximize rewards, and complete difficult purchases without manually searching many disconnected services.

The product should feel like a knowledgeable shopping advisor with rewards intelligence built in—not a coupon directory or shopping-portal table.

## Problem statement

Shopping research is fragmented across search engines, review sites, retailer pages, portal aggregators, credit-card portals, airline malls, coupon sites, and international forwarding services. Users must reconcile product quality, retailer trustworthiness, price, rewards, policies, and delivery themselves.

## Primary user

The initial user is an informed U.S. shopper who values product quality first, uses Chase Ultimate Rewards as a primary currency, also has access to Rakuten and airline shopping portals, and sometimes seeks specialty or international products.

## Jobs to be done

### Job 1 — Browse a category

“When I am loosely exploring a category, help me understand the retailer landscape and show available rewards immediately.”

Example: “Show me places that sell bath towels.”

### Job 2 — Research products

“When I need a recommendation, help me identify strong products and show where to buy them with rewards.”

Example: “What are the best carry-on bags under $250?”

### Job 3 — Optimize an exact product purchase

“When I already know the exact product, do not redirect me into discovery; identify the best credible purchase path and rewards.”

Example: “Where should I buy the Sony WH-1000XM6?”

### Job 4 — Compare retailers and rewards

“When I am comparing stores, explain assortment, quality positioning, price, reputation, shipping, returns, and rewards—not rewards alone.”

Example: “Compare Brooklinen and The Company Store.”

### Job 5 — Evaluate timing

“When current and historical data are available, help me judge whether a price or reward rate is unusually good and whether waiting may be reasonable.”

This job is future-facing until reliable historical data exists.

### Job 6 — Acquire difficult or international products

“When a product is not normally available in the United States, help me determine whether direct shipping, forwarding, or proxy purchasing is practical.”

## Product principles

1. Product quality outranks rewards.
2. Rewards are visible whenever retailers are discussed and reliable data can be found.
3. Compare retailers as businesses, not only as reward endpoints.
4. Never hide material tradeoffs.
5. Never fabricate dynamic rates, prices, inventory, or policy details.
6. Prefer public data and require minimal personal data.
7. Ask questions only when the answer would materially change.
8. Use progressive disclosure, but include rewards at the first useful level.
9. Surface niche retailers when they add genuine discovery value.
10. Stop when more research is unlikely to improve the decision.

## Success

The product succeeds when the user discovers better options, understands the tradeoffs, sees rewards without asking, spends less time researching, and can act confidently.

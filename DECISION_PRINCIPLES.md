# Decision Principles

These principles are the product constitution. Prompts, features, and architecture changes should be evaluated against them.

1. Product suitability outranks rewards.
2. Explicit user constraints outrank inferred preferences.
3. Rewards should be visible whenever retailers are discussed.
4. Rewards belong at the first useful disclosure layer.
5. Compare retailers beyond their reward rates.
6. Never fabricate rates, prices, inventory, policies, or delivery facts.
7. Dynamic facts require current verification or a clear caveat.
8. Public data is the default; private account access is not required for MVP value.
9. Targeted account offers must not be represented as universal.
10. Unlike currencies remain separate unless a transparent valuation model is applied.
11. The largest nominal multiplier is not automatically the best economic value.
12. Total price and product quality may outweigh rewards.
13. Authorized-seller status and retailer credibility matter.
14. Ask clarifying questions only when the answer would materially change.
15. Make low-risk assumptions rather than creating conversational friction.
16. Never ask whether the user wants rewards included.
17. Use only the capabilities needed for the request.
18. Exact-product requests should not trigger unnecessary product discovery.
19. Progressive disclosure should reduce clutter, not hide core rewards intelligence.
20. Surface niche retailers when they add real value.
21. Discovery value is a product objective.
22. Explain material tradeoffs.
23. Distinguish confirmed information from likely or unknown information.
24. Treat forwarding and proxy purchasing as different services.
25. International guidance must include return, warranty, customs, and compatibility implications when material.
26. Stop researching when more work is unlikely to change the recommendation.
27. A partial transparent answer is better than a comprehensive fabricated answer.
28. Specialist failure should not automatically fail the whole interaction.
29. Final responses must feel unified.
30. Product requirements should remain implementation-agnostic.
31. Do not add architecture until evaluation demonstrates a need.
32. Every major change should be recorded with rationale and tradeoffs.
